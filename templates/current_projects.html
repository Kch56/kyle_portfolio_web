Featured Data Science Project · Completed · September 2026

Under Pressure: Housing Affordability and Displacement Vulnerability

An exploratory study of housing affordability pressure and displacement vulnerability across Mecklenburg County census tracts and Charlotte neighborhoods.

Final Research Question

Which housing, demographic, and economic characteristics are associated with rent burden across Mecklenburg County census tracts, and what patterns of neighborhood displacement vulnerability appear in City of Charlotte data?

How the scope changed

The project started with a broader question about displacement and gentrification. I narrowed it to rent burden and the City's separate vulnerability classification because those are the outcomes the available data can support.

Completed analysis

I cleaned and merged public data, compared housing and demographic characteristics across census tracts, examined their associations with rent burden, and reviewed neighborhood-level City indicators separately.

Data sources

The study uses the 2024 American Community Survey five-year estimates and two City of Charlotte geographic data services.

What the results mean

The analysis highlights affordability patterns worth investigating. It does not prove displacement, estimate individual risk, or identify neighborhoods that have gentrified.

Featured Data Science Project · Completed · October 2026

Where Will Charlotte Crashes Cluster Next Month?

A machine-learning study that forecasts monthly reported crash counts across 500-meter grid cells and identifies areas that may deserve closer transportation-safety review.

Research Question

Can past crash patterns predict which 500-meter areas of Charlotte will have higher reported crash counts next month, and do separate morning and afternoon forecasts improve the hotspot picture?

Approach

I prepared spatial and time-based features, used chronological training, validation, and test periods, and compared two machine-learning models against a simple baseline.

All-day results

The top 5% of predicted grid cells captured 1,604 of 3,864 reported December crashes, or 41.5% of the total.

Time-of-day extension

The morning model captured 38.4% of reported crashes, while the afternoon model captured 42.6% within each period's top-ranked cells.

Responsible use

The forecasts rank areas for further review. They do not predict individual crashes or replace engineering analysis, field observations, or community knowledge.
